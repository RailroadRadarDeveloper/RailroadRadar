7. Safety

RailroadRadar is for viewing train information and sharing railfan reports. It is not an emergency service or a safety tool. Train positions, times, tracks, and alerts may be delayed, incomplete, or incorrect. A train that does not appear on the map may still be approaching. Never use RailroadRadar to decide whether it is safe to cross tracks, enter railroad property, or approach a train.

Stay Safe Around Trains

  • Stay off tracks and restricted railroad property. Do not walk on or alongside tracks or enter yards, rights-of-way, bridges, or tunnels unless you are specifically authorized. Trains cannot stop quickly and may be closer, faster, and quieter than you expect.
  • Cross only at designated crossings. Obey signals, gates, bells, and signs. Never go around or under a lowered gate. Wait until the crossing is clear and signals indicate it is safe.
  • On platforms, stay behind the marked safety line and follow posted instructions and railroad staff.
  • Observe and photograph trains only from places where you are permitted to be. Follow the railroad’s rules and keep a safe distance from tracks.
  • Do not use RailroadRadar while driving or allow your phone to distract you near tracks, crossings, or platforms.

Emergencies and Safety Information

If someone is in immediate danger or needs emergency assistance, call 911. For a problem at a railroad crossing, move yourself and others well away from the tracks first if it is safe to do so. Then use the railroad emergency number and crossing identification number posted on the blue-and-white Emergency Notification System (ENS) sign at that crossing. For other railroad safety concerns, use the contact information posted by the railroad or its official police or customer-service channel. Railroad contact details and procedures can change, so use current official information.

For general rail-safety information, visit Operation Lifesaver.
