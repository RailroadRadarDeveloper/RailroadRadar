7. Safety

As cool as trains are, they are fast, quieter than you think, and can kill. RailroadRadar is a map for following and riding trains. It is not a safety tool. By using the Service, you agree to follow this section.

Stay Safe Around Trains

  • Stay off tracks and railroad property. Walking on or along tracks, or going into yards, rights-of-way, bridges, or tunnels without permission, is trespassing. It is against the law, and it can kill you. Trains cannot stop quickly or swerve, and they are often closer, faster, and quieter than they seem.
  • Cross only at marked crossings. Obey all signals, gates, bells, and signs. Never go around or under a lowered gate. Expect a train on any track at any time; another train may be coming right after the first one passes.
  • On platforms, stay back from the edge. Stand behind the yellow line or edge markings, and follow posted rules and railroad staff.
  • Watch and take photos only from places you are allowed to be, such as public sidewalks, roads, parks, and the public areas of stations. Follow each railroad’s photography rules, and stay well back from the tracks.
  • Never use RailroadRadar while driving, and don’t let your phone distract you near tracks, at crossings, or on platforms.
  • Never rely on RailroadRadar for a safety decision. Train positions, times, tracks, and alerts on the map can be late, missing, or wrong, and a train that is not on the map may still be coming. Do not use the map to decide whether it is safe to be on or near tracks, or to cross them. Always use your own eyes and ears, and follow posted signals, signs, and railroad staff.

Where to Get Help

  • Emergencies: call 911.
  • A problem at a railroad crossing (for example, a vehicle stuck on the tracks, or a gate or signal that isn’t working): if anyone is on the tracks, get them off and well away first. Then call the railroad’s emergency number on the blue-and-white Emergency Notification System (ENS) sign at the crossing, and give the crossing number shown on the sign. If someone is hurt or in danger, call 911. For MBTA Commuter Rail crossings, you can also call the MBTA at 800-522-8236.
  • Railroad police, for crimes, suspicious activity, or safety concerns on their railroad (in an emergency, call 911 first):
    • MBTA Transit Police: 617-222-1212, or text 617-600-0683
    • Amtrak Police: 800-331-0008, or text APD11 (27311)
    • MTA Police (Metro-North and the Long Island Rail Road): 888-682-9117 for emergencies, or 212-878-1001
    • NJ TRANSIT Police: 973-378-6565 or 800-242-0236, or text NJTPD (65873)
    • Metra Police: 312-322-2800, or 312-322-2999 for non-emergencies
    For other railroads, call 911 or use the contact information posted at the station.
  • Rail safety tips: Operation Lifesaver, oli.org.
  • If you are thinking about suicide, or are worried about someone who might be, you are not alone, and help is available any time. In the U.S., call or text 988 to reach the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. In Canada, call or text 9-8-8. These services are free and confidential. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

These numbers belong to the organizations listed, which are not affiliated with RailroadRadar, and they can change. Check the organization’s official website if you are unsure.
